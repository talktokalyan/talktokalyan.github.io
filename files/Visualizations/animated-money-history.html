Clay accounting tokens

    What makes an object “money”?

    Test each example against money’s functions: medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value. Some examples were money in a local social system; others were accounting devices, credit instruments or ceremonial wealth.

    Important distinctions for the lecture

    • Clay tokens were primarily accounting counters—not automatically coins or general-purpose currency.
    • Rai stones could change ownership without being physically moved.
    • Mefo bills were state-guaranteed credit instruments, not circulating cash.
    • POW-camp cigarettes became a spontaneous local currency in conditions of scarcity.
